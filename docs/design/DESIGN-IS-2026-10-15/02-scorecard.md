# Scorecard (scored after the fixes in 01-evidence.md)

1. Good design is innovative — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: a familiar Now Playing card. The new part is that the remote never plays on the Mac and walks the user to the TV (status, ring around the AirPlay button, a one-line instruction).
   Justification: it refreshes a known pattern with a clear improvement for the AirPlay-only flow; nothing new beyond that.
2. Good design makes a product useful — Score: 3/3
   Evidence: welcome (1 action) → drop zone (1 action) → choose TV (1 action) → transport. No decoy actions. Every state offers the next step.
   Justification: the primary task is drop, pick the TV, watch, which is as few steps as macOS allows (it requires the TV to be picked each time).
3. Good design is aesthetic — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: system type and colours, one accent. Remaining: spacing isn't on a strict grid; progress 6 pt vs volume 4 pt.
   Justification: one visible system with two minor inconsistencies left.
4. Good design makes a product understandable — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: standard glyphs, all labelled. The lighter "converted" part of the track needed a tooltip ("Ready up to 35:00").
   Justification: one control needs a tooltip to be fully understood.
5. Good design is unobtrusive — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: the artwork and title lead. While choosing a TV, the instruction box and ring are deliberately assertive.
   Justification: chrome is visible but quiet; it is louder only while it is the next step.
6. Good design is honest — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: every label matches its behaviour after the fixes (scrubber stops at the converted edge; volume is inert until playing; the panel says Open). The tagline "Send any video on your Mac" is one inflation, since corrupt or audio-only files are refused.
   Justification: at most one minor inflation.
7. Good design is long-lasting — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: system controls and SF Symbols. The icon uses the Big Sur squircle and a gradient.
   Justification: one dated marker (the gradient icon), which is also the platform idiom.
8. Good design is thorough down to the last detail — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: empty, loading, error (with Dismiss), success, paused, TV disconnected, disabled and drag-over are all designed. Focus rings rely on system defaults for plain buttons, though every action has a shortcut.
   Justification: one state (focus) is rough.
9. Good design is environmentally friendly — Score: 3/3
   Evidence: a native 0.8 MB binary. No idle animation. Dark mode honoured. No network use except to the TV and the video's host. The 48 MB ffmpeg is the product's engine.
   Justification: it meets every anchor of the top level that applies to a native app.
10. Good design is as little design as possible — Score: 2/3
    Evidence: each element serves the task. Arguably removable: the instruction box while choosing a TV (the status and ring say it too) and the in-window Stop (the menu has it).
    Justification: two removable elements.

**Total: 22/30**
